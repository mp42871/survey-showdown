Game mode

Teams

With 3 or 4 teams, control rotates each round, and after three strikes the next team in order gets the steal.

Topics

Pick one or more topics. Rounds are drawn from the questions in the topics you pick.

Game

A player's round ends when they find every answer or use up their wrong answers. The round ends for everyone when time runs out, everyone is done, or you end it.

When time runs out, the team gets a strike. During a steal, running out of time means the steal fails. Tap the timer on the board to pause it.

How a round works

  1. Face-off. One player from each team comes up. Whoever names the higher answer wins control for their team. Tap that team's score panel.
  2. Play. The team in control takes turns guessing. Correct answers flip over and add to the round points.
  3. Three strikes. After three wrong answers, the other team huddles and gets one guess to steal every round point.
  4. Win. The team with the most points after the last round wins.

Hosting: tap a tile to reveal it, or type a student's guess in the box and the game checks it against the answers, so the host doesn't need to know them. Keyboard: 1–8 reveal, X strike, Z undo, N next round.

      Getting started

      1. Open the game on the classroom display and press Full screen.
      2. In Game mode, choose Two teams (classic face-off on the board) or Everyone plays (every student or group plays on their own computer).
      3. Tick one or more Topics, set the number of rounds and timers, and press Start new game (or Open the lobby).
      4. Everything is saved in this browser as you go. Use Save your work to keep a copy in Google Drive or a file.

      Two-team game

      1. Face-off: one player from each team answers. Tap the winning team's score panel to give them control, or turn on team buzzers so teams buzz in from their computers.
      2. Play: the team in control keeps guessing. Tap a tile to reveal it, or type the guess in the box and the game checks it for you, even with misspellings.
      3. Strikes and steals: three wrong answers, and the next team gets one guess to steal all the round points.
      4. Mistakes: press Undo to take back anything. If a correct guess was marked wrong, press Undo, then tap the right tile; that wording counts from then on.
      5. With 3 or 4 teams (Options), control rotates each round and the next team in order steals.

      Everyone plays

      1. Open the lobby. Students go to the web address on the screen, choose Join a game and type the code and their name.
      2. Each round, everyone answers the same question on their own computer. Correct answers fill in their board; wrong ones use up their strikes.
      3. The round ends when time runs out, everyone is done, or you press End round. Everyone then sees all the answers.
      4. After a round, tap a wrong guess under the board to accept it if it should have counted.
      5. With Play as: teams, students pick a team when they join and the team score is the average of its players, so team sizes don't matter.
      6. Tap ✕ next to a name on the leaderboard, then Remove?, to remove a player.

      Student computers

      Computers connect over the internet with the join code. No accounts are needed. Each computer needs the game's web address and internet access. New code makes a fresh code if the old one got shared too widely. A student who refreshes keeps their score.

      Host remote: on your phone, open the game, choose Join a game, tick I'm the teacher and type the code. Tap Allow on the big screen. Your phone shows the hidden answers and the main buttons.

      Topics & questions

      Press Edit topics and questions to make topics, add questions, and set each answer's points and other accepted wordings (Also accept). Answers are matched forgivingly: plurals, typos, word order and common synonyms count.

      Other ways to add questions: paste from a spreadsheet, use Make questions with AI, browse the topic library, or run a class survey to build boards from your students' real answers.

      Teaching tools

      • Explanation cards: add a short "why" to any answer in the editor. It appears when the answer is revealed and on students' screens after each round.
      • Mistake report: at the end of a game, see which answers were missed most and the most common wrong guesses.
      • Class survey: students answer your questions first; the app groups their answers and builds the boards.

      Running the game

      • Pause covers the board and stops the clocks, for interruptions.
      • Sound opens volume controls, background music and quiet mode.
      • Lightning round: tick it in Setup to finish with five quick questions against the clock.
      • Speed bonus: extra points for answering first.

      Saving & sharing

      • Save to Google Drive keeps your topics, settings, history and any game in progress in your own Drive.
      • Share this topic by link (in the editor) makes a link another teacher can open to add your topic.
      • Game history lists finished games with scores and mistake reports. Add a class or period name in Setup to tell them apart.
      • Share to Google Classroom posts the join link to a class.

      Troubleshooting

      • "Can't reach the connection service" or "No game found": check the code, and that the board shows the same code. Some school networks block this kind of connection; ask IT to allow it, or play on the board only.
      • Google sign-in blocked: school accounts may need IT to allow the app. Personal Gmail works.
      • Something looks stuck: refresh the page. Games, topics and scores are saved as you go.
      • A correct answer marked wrong: Undo and tap the tile (two teams), or accept it after the round (Everyone plays).

      Keyboard shortcuts (on the board)

      1–8 reveal an answer · X strike · Z undo · N next round · R reveal the rest · P pause · G type a guess. On a team computer, Space buzzes in.

      Describe a topic or paste your notes. Claude drafts survey-style questions with answers, points, other accepted wordings and short explanations. Check them before class: AI can make mistakes.

      Get a key at console.anthropic.com (usage is billed to that account; about a cent or two per batch). The key goes only from this browser to Anthropic. Don't save it on a shared computer.

      Import from a spreadsheet

      In Google Sheets or Excel, use columns Question, Answer, Points, Also accept, Explanation, one row per answer. Leave the question blank to keep adding answers to the one above. Select the cells, copy, and paste below, or open a .csv file.

      Up to 8 answers per question. "Also accept" lists other words that count when you type a guess (separate them with commas). The sample questions use made-up numbers; survey your own students for real ones. Questions save in this browser automatically.

      Copy or paste a whole topic

      One question per block, blank line between blocks. Each answer line is answer | points | also accept. Add x2 or x3 to the end of a question for bonus points.
